The safest route shouldn’t be a secret. This student led pilot is a community map of safer miles, made by the women who run them.

Before a walk or run, many women think beyond the distance.
Is the path well lit? Can you see what is ahead? Will other people be nearby?
These details can help you decide whether a route feels right today.

These concerns are common. In October 2023, Gallup reported that fifty-three percent of women in the United States were afraid to walk alone at night within a mile of home.
In a survey published in twenty twenty-three by Adidas and White Ribbon, ninety-two percent of women runners reported concerns about outdoor safety.
Clear local information helps people choose what feels right for them.

The map looks at six features of a place: foot traffic, staff nearby, clear sightlines, lighting, cell phone signal, and visible parking.
These details can help you compare routes. They cannot tell you exactly how a place will feel, but they give you useful information before you go.

Western North Carolina Safer Miles is a free map of ninety parks, greenways, and trails across six counties in Western North Carolina.
Each place has a score, and the sources are listed.
Ratings are anonymous. They describe places, not people.

Local knowledge helps the map stay useful.
Anyone can leave an anonymous rating, suggest a missing park or trail, or report a change.
If a route is unfamiliar, walk it with a friend or run club. Use the map’s safety check questions together.
Each contribution gives the next person more information to consider.

Use the map as a quick check, not a promise.
Scores are based on public information. Some locations have not been checked in person, and conditions can change.
If a score or detail makes you think, “Not today,” choose another route or go with someone you trust.
You decide what feels right.

Safe shouldn’t be something we find alone. Fear has taken enough of our miles.
Rate your favorite loop this week. Add a place we missed. Send this to a friend who runs alone. The more we use it, the more we know.
The next rating makes the next woman’s run a little less ambiguous. Run it. Rate it. Share it. Tap the button, and take your miles back.
