WNC Safer Miles

A community map of parks, greenways and trails across Western North Carolina, scored on what helps women feel safer running or walking alone. It starts in Buncombe, Henderson, Jackson, Haywood, Transylvania and Madison counties and grows as women rate and suggest places.

For all women, trans women included, and for nonbinary, gender-expansive and LGBTQ+ people who have ever felt unsafe out alone.

More about this pilot

This is a pilot, and the scores are starting estimates. A high score means fewer known risk factors, not a guarantee of safety. The starting scores come from a review of public sources, such as park and county pages, local news and Google Maps listings, not from site visits, and they are waiting for local women to confirm or correct them. Anyone can rate a place from its panel; it takes about 20 seconds and asks for no name. An email is optional, only if you want a reply. Use this as one input alongside your own judgment. This map is not an emergency service; if you are in danger, call 911.

If something happens

Being followed, watched or harassed, including for your gender, how you look or who you love, is reason enough to call. Advocates in every county on this map, text and chat lines, and LGBTQ+ support, kept on one page so it is quick to find and quick to leave.

Better, together

Why community works, and five ways to help

This map works the way gadugi does: people joining together for one another's good, doing what no one could do alone. Every rating and suggestion makes it a little truer for the next woman.

Why together works

  • Strength in numbersNeighborhoods where people trust each other and are willing to step in for one another have less violence. Sampson, Raudenbush and Earls, Science, 1997 ↗
  • Moving together calms the bodyIn one small study, people who exercised in a group had a 26% drop in stress; those who exercised alone saw no real change. Yorks and colleagues, 2017 ↗
  • Time outside is worth protectingPeople who spent at least two hours a week in nature, in one visit or several, were more likely to report good health and wellbeing. White and colleagues, Scientific Reports, 2019 ↗

Five ways to make it better

  1. Rate the places you useYour body's read on a place is information. If somewhere feels off, or feels good, say so. It takes about 20 seconds and asks for no name. Choose a place
  2. Suggest a place we're missingEvery suggestion is researched before it goes on the map. Suggest a place ↗
  3. Walk it togetherGo with a friend or two at the hour you'd usually go. Notice the lighting, how far ahead you can see and where you'd head if you needed to leave, the same questions women's safety audits ask. Then rate it.
  4. Tell us what's changedA light out, a new closure, a trail cleared after Helene. Report a change ↗
  5. Leave it better than you found itPack out your own trash and one piece that isn't yours. A cared-for place is a watched place. Caring for the land

Who made this

WNC Safer Miles was created by Alison Rose, a runner in Asheville who studies psychology at UNC Asheville, with minors in American Indian and Indigenous Studies and in Women’s, Gender, and Sexuality Studies, on her way to clinical neuropsychology. Here in Western North Carolina she serves as a crisis responder with TIP of WNC ↗, has advocated for children as a Guardian ad Litem, and invented ANCHOR, tactile grounding tools for nervous system regulation now being piloted in an ICU in the region. Her work keeps returning to one question: what helps a body feel safe? This map asks it at the scale of a community.
